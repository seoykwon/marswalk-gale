# Marswalk

## 1. What it is

Marswalk is an interactive web map for planning an astronaut's walking traverse (EVA) in Gale Crater, Mars. It stacks NASA and USGS data on one map: MRO CTX and HiRISE imagery, a 1 m HiRISE terrain model, Mars Odyssey THEMIS thermal imagery, MGS MOLA topography, and Curiosity's own RAD radiation and REMS weather measurements. It turns them into numbers a planner can act on: distance, walking time, climb, slope hazards, comms line of sight, radiation dose and ground-temperature swing for two routes (route A, a flat walk from Bradbury Landing to Yellowknife Bay; route B, a steeper route on the lower slopes of Mount Sharp). A second view reads the same data for the electronics carried on the walk. It is a static site (Leaflet, pre-cut tiles, no server logic) and works offline after the first visit.

**Live site: https://seoykwon.github.io/marswalk-gale/**  
**Repository: https://github.com/seoykwon/marswalk-gale**

## 3. Key findings

All numbers come from `site/data/site_facts.json` (generated by `scripts/61_site_facts.py`).

- Route B is 1,824.5 m one way (3,649.1 m round trip) with +158.6 m of climb. Round trip with a 30 min stay: 2 h 34 min at an assumed 2.5 km/h.
- Route B has 3 stretches steeper than 15°, 28.0 m in total. Its steepest DEM slope is 17.4°. The largest rover tilt on the route is 24.0° (sol 4970).
- Radiation, MSL RAD, sols 4613-4843 (2025-2026): mean 6.898 µGy/h in the silicon detector B and 6.434 µGy/h in the tissue-equivalent detector E (19,502 valid observations over 231 sols).
- Radiation, sols 1-359 (2012-2013): mean 9.029 µGy/h (detector B). The two periods are 13 years apart.
- Route B round-trip dose, recent period: 17.7 µGy (detector B), 16.5 µGy (detector E).
- Ground temperature, MSL REMS, sols 4175-4843 (one Mars year): mean daily range 83.1 K (min 59.8 K, max 111.0 K). Fastest mean heating +17.4 K/h at 08:00 LMST, fastest cooling -14.2 K/h at 17:00 LMST. Mean time below -40 °C: 14.3 h per sol.
- Route B comms line of sight is lost over 856.0 m of the route, with a 2 m antenna (assumption).

## 4. How to run locally

```
cd site && python3 -m http.server 8765
```

Then open http://localhost:8765/. Opening the HTML files directly with `file://` does not work: the pages load data, tiles and the service worker over HTTP. Python 3 only; no packages are needed.

## 5. How the data was built

Scripts in `scripts/`, in run order. Run each as `python3 -I scripts/<name>`.

1. `10_unify_crs.sh` - puts every source raster on one Mars sphere lat/lon frame (virtual rasters only; originals are not changed).
2. `11_check_alignment.py` - checks the rasters against Curiosity's surveyed waypoints.
3. `20_parse_rad.py` - MSL RAD RDR files to `rad.json` (dose rates, LET spectra, spikes).
4. `21_parse_rems.py` - MSL REMS MODRDR to `rems_A.json` and `rems_B.json`; uses `ls_mars24.py` for Ls.
5. `22_vectors.py` - traverse, waypoints, sites, places and photos.
6. `23_geology_rudolph.py` - Rudolph et al. unit lines to `geology_rudolph.geojson`.
7. `30_tiler.py` and `31_tile_areas.py` - cut map tiles and write `layers.json`.
8. `32_dem_derive.py` - hillshade, slope, elevation colour, contours from the 1 m DEM.
9. `33_viewshed.py` - line-of-sight layers from a base.
10. `34_grid.py` - 10 m numeric grids for the draw tool.
11. `35_slope_check.py` - DEM slope against Curiosity tilt.
12. `40_route_build.py` and `40_route_profile.py` - build routes A and B and write `routes.json`.
13. `44_route_validate.py` - route validation.
14. `50_export_config.py` - exports the assumptions the app shows.
15. `55_eva_precache.py` - lists files for offline use.
16. `61_site_facts.py` - writes `site_facts.json`, the only source of numbers on the site pages.

All assumptions live in `scripts/config.json`.

## 7. Assumptions and limits

- **The dose budget is a planning number, not a health limit.** The EVA dose budget (50 µGy, detector E) is an assumption used to compare routes. It is not a medical or regulatory limit.
- **Sol 4716 was a transient dose-rate event of unconfirmed cause.** Its peak was 11.95 µGy/h against a sol mean of 7.86 µGy/h, rising over about 7 h. The cause is not established here.
- **The route A and route B dose rates were measured 13 years apart** (2012-2013 and 2025-2026). The difference between them mixes location and time, and is not a clean route comparison.
- **Only Chrome has been tested.** Other browsers are untested.
- Assumptions (not from NASA data): walking speed 2.5 km/h; slope caution 15° and danger 25°; EVA length at most 6 h with a 30 min reserve and a 30 min stay at the destination; daylight ends at 17.5 h LMST; minimum ground temperature -40 °C; antenna height 2 m.
- RAD records no position. The rover stayed within a few hundred metres, so radiation is shown as a time series at the rover, not as a spatial map.
- Thermal values describe the exposed ground, not the temperature inside equipment.
- Ground-temperature data use only high-confidence rows (confidence suffix 111); mean long-term uncertainty is 9.07 K.
- RAD data exclude observations shorter than 600 s or with non-zero QC flags.
- The map is not a flight-qualified tool.

## 10. AI-use disclosure

**Use of AI tools (called Super Intelligence, SI, in the 2026 Space Apps rules).** Marswalk was built with Claude Code, an AI coding assistant from Anthropic. The planning was done with Claude Fable 5.2. The implementation was done with Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet. Claude Code wrote and edited the project's source code (the web pages under `site/` and the data-processing scripts under `scripts/`), wrote the planning and review notes under `Plan/`, and drafted the text of the interface and of this documentation. The data processing and the checks were run by those scripts in this repository, not by the assistant by hand: the scripts read the NASA and USGS source files listed above and produced the map tiles, route profiles and numbers shown on the site. The numbers shown on the site come from those source files and scripts; the assistant did not supply measurements. The idea of how to solve the challenge is the project owner's: a layered map of Gale Crater, built from NASA mission data, for planning a walking EVA, with an astronaut-facing view and the same data read for its impact on electronics. The owner chose the challenge, set the direction, and reviewed and approved the output. The AI assistant was used as a tool to plan and carry out the implementation; its working notes are kept in Plan/. Assumptions that are not from NASA data (for example antenna height and slope hazard classes) are labelled as assumptions in the app. No images, video or audio on the site were generated by AI. Images on the site are NASA mission data or screenshots of the app itself. No generated content uses NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
