## Project name

Marswalk Gale — an EVA route planner for Gale Crater

## High-level summary

Marswalk Gale is an interactive web map for planning an astronaut's walking traverse (EVA) in Gale Crater, Mars. It stacks data from several NASA missions on one map — MRO CTX and HiRISE imagery, a 1 m HiRISE terrain model, Mars Odyssey THEMIS thermal imagery, MGS MOLA topography, and Curiosity's own measurements from its RAD radiation detector and REMS weather station — and turns them into numbers a planner can act on. Click a route and the panel reports distance, walking time, climb, the length of slopes above 15° and 25°, the radiation dose for the trip, and the ground-temperature swing the suit and equipment must survive. Two routes are provided: a flat reference walk from Bradbury Landing to Yellowknife Bay, and a steeper route on the lower slopes of Mount Sharp, where Curiosity measured radiation and weather in 2025–26.

A second lens reads the same data from the point of view of the electronics carried on the walk. Curiosity's RAD has a silicon detector, so its dose rate is directly the dose a silicon chip receives. We use it to put total-ionizing-dose margins and single-event-effect risk in context, and REMS ground-temperature data to show the daily thermal cycling that stresses solder joints and packages.

## Project details (status at submission)

What it does
- Layer toggles grouped as base imagery / terrain / surface / Curiosity measurements / EVA plan
- Slope hazard layer computed from the HiRISE 1 m DEM (thresholds 15° and 25°, stated as assumptions)
- Route summary panel: distance, time, climb, slope-hazard segments, radiation dose for the trip, temperature swing, comms line-of-sight
- Radiation panel from MSL RAD: multi-sol dose-rate statistics, daily cycle, and dose-rate spikes
- Weather panel from MSL REMS: daily ground-temperature range across a full Mars year
- Curiosity traverse, waypoints and raw rover photos placed along the path

Method
- All rasters are put on a common Mars 2000 sphere lat/lon frame and checked against Curiosity's surveyed waypoints before use
- RAD data parsed directly from PDS RDR files; observations with non-zero QC flags or durations under 600 s are excluded
- REMS data from PDS MODRDR products, hourly averages, high-confidence ground-temperature rows only
- Static site (Leaflet) with pre-cut map tiles; no server needed

Honest limits
- RAD records no position, and the rover stayed within a few hundred metres during the measurement period, so the map shows radiation as measured at the rover's location over time, not as a spatial heat map
- Walking speed, slope thresholds and antenna height are assumptions, shown in the app and adjustable
- Thermal values describe the exposed environment, not the internal temperature of equipment

Status at this submission: data acquisition and processing are complete or in progress; the web map is under active development. The final version will be linked from this page.

## Use of AI

AI coding assistants (Anthropic Claude) were used to help plan the work, write data-processing scripts, and draft code and text. All data come from NASA and USGS archives listed below. Numeric results were checked against independent recomputation.

## NASA data used

- MRO CTX Gale Crater mosaic (via NASA JPL Mars Trek)
- MRO HiRISE 1 m DEM mosaic of Gale Crater (via Mars Trek / USGS Astrogeology)
- MRO HiRISE colour orthomosaic of the MSL area (USGS Astrogeology)
- MSL RAD Reduced Data Records, MSL-M-RAD-3-RDR-V1.0 (PDS Planetary Plasma Interactions Node)
- MSL REMS Modeled RDR, mslrem_1001 (PDS Atmospheres Node)
- MSL traverse and waypoints (NASA/JPL "Where is Curiosity?" map)
- MSL raw images (mars.nasa.gov raw image archive)
- Mars Odyssey THEMIS day/night IR and thermal inertia (USGS Astrogeology / Mars Trek)
- MGS MOLA global DEM and MGS TES products (USGS Astrogeology / Mars Trek)
- USGS SIM 3292 Global Geologic Map of Mars; IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature
