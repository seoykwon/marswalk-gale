Electronics impact: what the rover measured

Curiosity's radiation detector (RAD) and weather station (REMS) recorded the environment at the two route sites. This page reads those records the way an electronics designer would: total ionizing dose, single-event effects and thermal cycling. It shows measured values only. It quotes no component tolerance numbers; comparisons with parts are qualitative.

Sentences marked General background are standard engineering knowledge, stated without a source on this site. Every number comes from the measurements.

Total ionizing dose (TID)

RAD's detector B is silicon, so its absorbed dose is the dose a silicon device on the rover deck receives. Sols 4613–4843 (2025–2026, route B site) averaged 6.898 µGy/h. Over one Earth year that is 60.5 mGy = 6.05 rad(Si).

Sols 1–359 (2012–2013, route A site) averaged 9.029 µGy/h, or 79.1 mGy per Earth year. The two periods are 13 years apart, so the difference is not read as an effect of altitude or place.

A device carried on one route B EVA (2 h 34 min) would collect 17.7 µGy(Si) at the recent mean rate.

General background TID shifts transistor thresholds and raises leakage as dose accumulates. A designer compares the mission dose with the TID rating on the part's datasheet, in krad(Si). This page states no such rating.

Sol 4716: transient event, cause unconfirmed

On sol 4716 the detector E sol mean rose to 7.86 µGy/h, with a largest single observation of 11.95 µGy/h, after a rise of about 7 h. It is the only sol of the period above the transient-event threshold. The cause has not been checked against solar-activity records, so it is called a transient event here, not a solar particle event.

General background For electronics, the concern during such an event is the higher particle flux, which raises the chance of single-event effects, more than the small extra total dose.

Single-event effects (SEE)

RAD also records the LET (linear energy transfer) spectrum in detector B. For sols 4613–4843 it has 52 bins. The relative density peaks in the 0.283 keV/µm bin and falls steeply toward high LET: high-LET particles are present, but rare. The chart is R5 in the map's Radiation tab.

General background An SEE is caused by one energetic particle, not by accumulated dose. A designer places a part's heavy-ion test results (its LET threshold and cross-section) against a spectrum like this one to judge how often upsets could occur.

This page computes no SEE rate. The spectrum gives shape only, with no absolute flux, and no part data is included.

Thermal cycling

REMS ground temperature at the route B site (the most recent Mars year, 561 sols with near-complete daily coverage) goes through one cycle per sol: 669 cycles per Mars year. The daily swing of hourly means averaged 83.1 K (smallest 59.8 K, largest 111.0 K).

The fastest mean warming is +17.4 K/h in the hour starting 08:00 LMST. The fastest mean cooling is −14.2 K/h in the hour starting 17:00. On average 14.3 h per sol are below −40 °C. That temperature is a comparison line only, not a part grade.

General background Each swing strains solder joints and packages because materials expand by different amounts. Larger and more frequent swings bring fatigue sooner. Enclosures and heaters reduce what the electronics actually see.

What this data can and cannot say

  • Detector B is silicon: it is used here for the equipment dose. Detector E is a plastic scintillator; used here as the body-side reading. It is shown on the chart for comparison (sols 4613–4843 mean 6.434 µGy/h), not used for electronics numbers.
  • RAD sits on the rover deck. The dose inside an equipment box depends on its shielding.
  • Absorbed dose (Gy, rad) only. No health limits are discussed.
  • No radiation map and no altitude dependence: RAD files carry no rover position.
  • Ground temperature is the environment an exposed surface sees, not the temperature inside equipment. Its mean long-term sensor uncertainty is 9.07 K.
  • Both records are short slices of the solar cycle and of Mars seasons.
  • No component tolerance numbers, temperature grades or SEE rates are given.
